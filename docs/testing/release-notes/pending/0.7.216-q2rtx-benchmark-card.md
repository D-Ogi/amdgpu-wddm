# Pending release-notes lines: ray tracing benchmark card (control app)

This file is not a release. It holds the tester-facing lines for the next tester release notes. The release
step copies the lines into `docs/testing/release-notes/<version>-tester.N.md` and deletes this file.

## New

- The Performance page has a new card, "Ray tracing benchmark". It measures how fast your card traces light and
  shows the result in pictures a second. It needs Quake II RTX on the computer: the free game of NVIDIA, from
  Steam or from the maker's own page. The driver package does not hold the game and never downloads it. The card
  finds a Steam copy by itself, and you can also choose the folder yourself. It says plainly when a folder holds
  no game, no game data, game data it cannot read, or a copy without the test scene.

- One run measures both ways your card can trace light, one after the other, because a game can ask for either
  and the speed differs. The card names them "First way" and "Second way" and gives the technical name of each
  under it. Each way takes about twenty seconds on our own board. The game opens in a small window of its own,
  1280 by 720, and closes again when it has finished. The card shows the pictures a second, the highest
  temperature and the average clock of each way, with the date and the driver release of the run.

- The line above the results says where the test stands: the way it measures now, the test you stopped, or the
  newest run saved on this computer after you open the application again. "Earlier runs" holds the runs before
  the one above it.

- The test stops by itself when the chip stays at 87 degrees, and after three minutes for each way, and "Stop the
  test" ends it at once. It says which of those happened instead of showing a number. On a card whose temperature
  the driver cannot read, the card says so and only the three minutes of each way are left.

- The last ten runs stay on this computer, so you can see whether a new driver release changed the number. On a
  BC-250 the card also shows what we measured on our own board, for comparison: 57.8 to 69.7 pictures a second at
  1280 by 720, with the release the card names next to them. The card says why a run lands anywhere inside that
  band: the chip raises its speed while the scene runs, and the scene takes about ten seconds.

- The card holds still while a run goes on. "Start the test" and "Stop the test" stay where they are from the
  first picture to the last, also when another card of the page gets a line in the meantime.

## Known behaviour

- The test changes nothing in your copy of the game: it passes every setting on the command line and writes the
  game's log into the driver's own folder. Your own settings, saved games and key bindings stay as they are.

- Leave the game alone while it measures, and do not start a second copy of it: another window in front of it
  changes the number.

- The numbers of two different screens, driver releases or copies of the game are not comparable. Compare runs of
  the same computer.
